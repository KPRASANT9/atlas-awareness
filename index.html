How to read this: themes, not rankings

Energy and Wisdom describe what the words of a song talk about and how hard they push. They are not scores of countries, cultures or peoples. A calm anthem is not better, and a fiery one is not worse.

Many anthems were written during a war, a revolution or a coronation, and their words carry that moment. Everything is read from English translations (with original-language roots for 159 songs), and translation is the biggest source of uncertainty: a typical 90% range spans about three of the atlas's five levels. Read every number with its range.

No country is highlighted anywhere on this site unless you choose it. Lists are alphabetical, never sorted by score.

The story: Geyam and Geetham

India has two national songs with equal honour. The Jaateeya Geetham, the national anthem, is Tagore's Jana Gana Mana. The Jaateeya Geyam, the national song, is Bankim Chandra Chatterjee's Vande Mataram. They are often described as two life philosophies: the Geyam as heart, devotion and ecology; the Geetham as conscience, unity and destiny.

The atlas asked whether that difference shows up when the two songs are placed among all the world's anthems, read the same way, theme by theme.

Jaateeya Geyam · national song

Vande Mataram

Its words dwell on landscape and on the land as mother, among the strongest in the world on both. The root of vande, Sanskrit √vand, means "to bow, revere", which the English "praise" loses. Its nearest neighbour worldwide is Bangladesh's Amar Shonar Bangla, another Bengal-renaissance song of mother-earth devotion.

Family: Garden & Mother. Energy −0.12 [−1.05, +0.86] (typical); Wisdom −0.93 [−1.75, −0.14]. Its low Wisdom is not a judgement: devotion and the sacred land are deliberately on neither axis.

Jaateeya Geetham · national anthem

Jana Gana Mana

A hymn of address to a guiding presence, "dispenser of India's destiny". It rouses by waking and hailing rather than marching: jāge ("it wakes"), gāhe tava jaya-gāthā ("sings thy victory song") and the closing jaya he, jaya he. It has one of the highest Awakening scores in the dataset.

Family: Crown & Covenant. Energy +1.25 [−0.04, +2.44] (leans rousing); Wisdom +1.44 [+0.70, +2.11] (more truth, virtue and future language than typical).

In the combined space the two are about as related as two randomly chosen songs. They are complementary, not overlapping: one loves a place, the other addresses a destiny. Open India's full spectrum to see all five Indian national-level songs side by side.

Key findings

  • Where you are shapes which themes you sing, not how hardSub-region explains 16–26% of each theme, but none of Energy once group count is adjusted for. Wisdom has a modest sub-regional geography (13.5%).
  • Five loose families, not tribesCall to Arms, Heritage Homeland, Unity & Justice, Garden & Mother and Crown & Covenant overlap heavily. Songs form a continuum with a broad ceremonial middle and four corners.
  • Two ways to rouseEnergy counts calls to struggle and action (Mobilisation) and waking, hailing and song (Awakening) equally. A hymn that hails can be as rousing as a march.
  • National songs are gentler than anthemsIn 16 of 20 countries with both, the national song has lower Energy (p = 0.03). The gap is in Awakening: anthems hail and acclaim more; they do not necessarily march harder.
  • Era leaves a fingerprintAnthems adopted before 1946 carry far more martial content than those adopted 1946–1989; monarch content falls steadily over time.
  • Translation is the biggest uncertaintyAn independent second translation keeps 82% of songs within one level on both axes, but the median 90% range still spans 1.8 SD of Energy. Original-language roots narrow it.
